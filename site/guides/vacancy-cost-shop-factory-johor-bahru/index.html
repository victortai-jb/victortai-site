Guide for landlords

How much does an empty shop or factory really cost in Johor Bahru?

Every month a shop or factory sits empty, you lose the full month's rent plus your holding costs. If you turn down a tenant to wait for RM500 more a month on a 3-year lease, you only come out ahead if the better tenant arrives within about three months.

By Victor Tai (Tai Sing Thong), REN 26739, The Roof Realty · Updated 2026-11-19

The mistake most landlords make.

Landlords compare two rents: the offer in hand and the rent they hope to get. They forget the third number, which is time.

An empty unit earns nothing, but the costs keep running: loan interest, quit rent, assessment, service charges for stratified units, insurance and basic maintenance. An empty factory also starts to deteriorate.

A simple way to calculate it.

Break-even waiting time (months) = (rent you hope for − rent offered) × lease months ÷ rent you hope for.

Example, a terrace factory:

Tenant's offer
RM5,000 a month
Rent you hope for
RM5,500 a month
Difference
RM500 a month
Lease
3 years (36 months)
Extra income over the lease
RM500 × 36 = RM18,000
Break-even waiting time
RM18,000 ÷ RM5,500 ≈ 3.3 months

If the RM5,500 tenant takes longer than about 3.3 months to appear, accepting RM5,000 today would have earned you more. That is before holding costs, which make the real break-even shorter still.

These figures illustrate the method. They are not market rents. Use your own numbers.

What changes the answer.

  • Lease length. A longer lease makes a higher rent worth more, so you can afford to wait a little longer. A one-year lease rarely justifies waiting.
  • How fast your type of unit rents out. A ground-floor shop on a busy road may rent in weeks. A large detached factory in a quieter industrial area can take many months. Ask an agent who handles that area what recent vacancy times have been.
  • Free renovation period. Commercial and industrial tenants often ask for one to three months rent-free to fit out. Count it as a cost in the comparison.
  • Tenant quality. A tenant who pays on time for three years is worth more than one who pays RM300 more and leaves after a year.

When holding out makes sense.

  1. Your asking rent is supported by recent deals for similar units nearby, not just by listings.
  2. The unit type is in short supply in that area.
  3. You can carry the holding costs without strain.

If none of these is true, the offer in hand is usually the better deal.
